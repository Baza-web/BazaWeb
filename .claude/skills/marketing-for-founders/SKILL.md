# Marketing for Founders

A practical, link-rich playbook for getting the first 10 / 100 / 1,000 users for a SaaS, app, or startup — built for technical founders without a marketing budget or team. This is reference material, not a script: read the relevant section(s) below, pull out the tactics that actually fit the product and stage in front of you, and say so plainly when something doesn't apply.

**Source & license**: adapted from [EdoStra/marketing-for-founders](https://github.com/EdoStra/marketing-for-founders) (Edoardo Stradella), licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. The full curated link list lives in `references/resources.md` — this file is the "which section do I need" map plus how to apply it.

## How to use this skill

1. Figure out what the user actually needs: a launch channel, ad copy, a pricing model, a landing page rewrite, a way to find their first 10 customers, etc. Don't dump the whole resource list — pick the 1-3 sections in `references/resources.md` that match, read them, and synthesize concrete next steps.
2. Always ground advice in the product and stage in front of you. "Where do I launch?" has a different answer for a pre-launch idea than for a product with 50 paying users.
3. Prefer free/organic tactics before paid ones for anything pre-revenue or pre-PMF — most of this list is organized that way on purpose (see `references/resources.md`'s own framing: inspirational VC-scale advice is rarely actionable for a bootstrapped founder).
4. When the user gives you a live product/site to work with, don't just link out — actually draft the thing (the Product Hunt post, the cold email, the pricing page copy, the ad headline) using the frameworks referenced, then point to the deeper guide if they want more.

## Applying this to a B2B SaaS tool (e.g. a vertical-specific tool like an industry ERP)

For a narrow, vertical B2B SaaS (sold to a specific trade/industry rather than a mass consumer audience), weight these sections higher than the others, in roughly this order of effort-to-payoff for a pre-revenue/early-revenue product:
1. **Idea Validation / User Research** — talk to a handful of real businesses in the target trade before spending on anything else.
2. **Sales & Cold Outreach** — a narrow ICP means direct outreach (and industry-specific communities/forums/Facebook groups, trade associations, trade shows) often outperforms broad social/content for the first handful of customers.
3. **Landing Pages, Messaging and Positioning** — the homepage needs to speak the trade's own language (its terminology, its specific pain points), not generic SaaS copy.
4. **Content Marketing + SEO + LLM SEO** — industry-specific how-to content compounds and is realistic to rank for a narrow niche, unlike competing for generic SaaS keywords.
5. **Places To Launch** (Product Hunt, directories) — useful for visibility/backlinks/social proof, but expect most signups from a broad launch to be outside the real target market; don't over-index on vanity signups.
6. **Ads** — hold off until messaging/positioning is validated by at least some organic/outbound traction; paid budget spent on unvalidated messaging is the single most common way early-stage founders burn cash (see the Ads section's framing in `references/resources.md`).
7. **Affiliates and Referrals / Influencer Marketing** — often most effective once there's a visible, credible first customer (or the founder's own existing business in that trade) to use as a case study/reference.
